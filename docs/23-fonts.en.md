> **13.3 Selection Advice**
> - **Lowest-risk for Chinese text**: Source Han Sans, Source Han Serif, LXGW WenKai, Smiley Sans — true SIL OFL 1.1, free for commercial use, modifiable, distributable with your product, no attribution.
> - **Big-corp free-for-commercial fonts**: Alibaba PuHuiTi 3.0 (7 SC weights, GB18030-2022 compliant), ZCOOL's 8 fonts (Premium Black / KuHei / KuaiLe / QingKe Butter etc.).
> - **UI / screen (Latin)**: Inter + Roboto + IBM Plex — any one of the three is almost always safe.
> - **Coding**: JetBrains Mono, Fira Code, Cascadia Code, Iosevka.
> - ⚠️ **ZCOOL is a "brand public-welfare license", not an open-source license**: commercial use is allowed, but **renaming is forbidden, re-distributing font files is forbidden, and profiting from the font itself is forbidden** — far stricter than OFL.
> - ⚠️ **MiSans requires explicitly crediting "MiSans" in your software**, and forbids adaptation and standalone distribution.
> - ❌ **Microsoft YaHei, PingFang, Founder / Hanyi commercial fonts**: bundled with the OS ≠ free to use commercially; downloadable ≠ licensed.

> **Notes on multi-script fonts**
> 1. **One family often spans multiple scripts**: the Noto / SIL series are mostly "one family covering an entire script family". For example, a single Noto Sans family includes Cyrillic and Greek extensions; Gentium Plus includes Latin, Greek and IPA; Andika includes Cyrillic and IPA. Check family coverage when selecting to avoid duplicate entries.
> 2. **Vertical Mongolian rendering depends on the OS/software**: traditional Uyghur-style Mongolian is **vertical (top-to-bottom, columns right-to-left)** and needs a shaping engine with vertical-writing support such as HarfBuzz; browser/editor support for vertical Mongolian varies, so always test before release. Cyrillic Mongolian uses ordinary Cyrillic letters and lays out horizontally — it is not the same font.
> 3. **RTL and vertical direction**: Arabic, Hebrew and Persian are all **RTL (right-to-left)** — UI and documents need `dir="rtl"` plus bidirectional (bidi) layout; Uyghur/Kazakh/Kyrgyz also use the Arabic script and are RTL. Wrong direction settings will misplace entire text blocks.
> 4. **Devanagari conjuncts**: Devanagari, Bengali, Tamil, Thai, Myanmar and other Indic/Southeast-Asian scripts have many **consonant conjuncts, vowel matras and upper/lower combining marks** — you must use an engine with OpenType complex text shaping (HarfBuzz / Uniscribe / CoreText / DirectWrite), or you get "tofu" boxes or wrong stacking.
> 5. **Vendor declarations can be revoked — archive them**: the "free for commercial use" statements of UKIJ, Qomolangma, Menksoft and similar vendors/institutions may change at any time. When including them, **save a snapshot / web archive of the license page (e.g. archive.org)** and keep the original font license inside your product for traceability.
> 6. **Minority-script Unicode ranges and coverage completeness**: Yi (U+A000–A4FF), Mongolian (U+1800–18AF), Tibetan (U+0F00–0FFF), Uyghur/Arabic (U+0600–06FF) occupy different ranges; some fonts cover only the base block and **lack extended characters or variation selectors (VS)** — confirm target characters fall within the font's coverage before mixing scripts.
> 7. **Distinguish "free for commercial use" from "free for personal use only"**: this list is mainly SIL OFL 1.1 (commercial use, modification and distribution with your product allowed, no attribution, except you may not sell the font files alone); ParaType Free Font License, Culmus free license and the rastikerdar Persian series (Vazirmatn / Samim etc., OFL) are all commercial-use-OK; GPL-class fonts (Tibetan Machine Uni, Baekmuk) are commercial-use-OK but copyleft — modifications and distributions must stay open-source; anything not 100% confirmed is marked "⚠️待核实" in the Highlight column — double-check before commercial use.
